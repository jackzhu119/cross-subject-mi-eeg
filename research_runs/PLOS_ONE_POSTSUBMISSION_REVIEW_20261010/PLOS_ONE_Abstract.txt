We examined the reliability of source-only training-duration selection and the external performance of complete fixed spectral-sharing pipelines for motor-imagery EEG decoding. An exploratory development study used nine BNCI2014_001 participants, excluding both sessions of each held-out person from fitting and duration selection. Separate frozen binary pipelines were evaluated on PhysioNet, Cho2017 and Lee2019 without target fitting or adaptation. A post-outcome BNCI sensor analysis described baseline-relative mu/beta power without decoder feedback. Mean-rank selection increased development balanced accuracy from 33.72% to 42.67%; S3 and S8 contributed 87.57% of the aggregate gain. A matched-runtime fixed-duration control improved four of nine people despite a positive mean. Shared-minus-broad external differences were +0.573 percentage points on PhysioNet (95% person-bootstrap interval [−0.097,1.242]), −1.513 on Cho2017 [−2.249,−0.804] and +0.204 on Lee2019 [−0.515,0.969]. These contrasts combine unequal source-selected durations with different input views and computation; they do not isolate spectral sharing. Archived CSP outputs showed strong left-prediction dominance on both later cohorts. The BNCI analysis found negative mean signed hand laterality with participant variation; its nine-person associations do not establish a decoder mechanism. Duration choices yielded large but concentrated development gains. The frozen shared-input pipelines showed no consistent external advantage, and uncertain contrasts establish neither superiority nor equivalence. Acquisition uncertainty and offline evaluation limit deployment conclusions.
