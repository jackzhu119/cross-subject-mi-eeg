## Proposed Introduction contribution paragraph

We investigate whether source training can improve the class that is harder to
decode for an unseen participant. The proposed objective combines
participant–class tail-risk regularization with predictive consistency across
three spectral views of the same trial. A matched five-method experiment
compares the combination with balanced empirical risk minimization, GroupDRO,
and each component alone. Participant-disjoint source validation determines
checkpoint selection, while fixed-duration and source-selected external
evaluations distinguish training-objective effects from duration effects.
Participant-level weaker-class recall, balanced accuracy and class-specific
recalls characterize both the average response and its distribution across
people.

## Methods

### Study design and datasets

The experiment will use BNCI2014-001 and the eligible two-class Yang2025 subset
as source cohorts, comprising nine and 51 participants, respectively. Their
eligible trial counts are 2,592 and 30,591. Dreyer2023 will supply exploratory
external evaluation of 85 eligible participants and 6,800 trials. Participant
identities are namespaced by dataset, and all sessions of a participant remain
together in every training, validation or test split. Source and external roles
will be recorded before model fitting.

Raw files will be recovered from the fixed official inventories and verified
against their registered object identities. The existing eligible-v3 rules will
define class mapping, timing, montage eligibility and retained trials. In
particular, the previously established Dreyer montage exclusions will remain
fixed. Array preparation on the school HPC system will constitute a separately
recorded numerical version. The original eligible-v3 outcomes will retain their
historical code and runtime identity.

### Signal representation

Each trial will contain broad, mu and beta views in an array of dimensions
3 × 18 × 320. The 18 shared scalp channels will use the established
common-average reference. The broad, mu and beta passbands are 8–30, 8–13 and
13–30 Hz, respectively. The vendored preprocessing implementation preserves
the frozen second-order-section coefficients and initial states, odd-padding
length 27, resampling to 160 Hz, and the [0.5, 2.5) s analysis-anchor window.
The Yang event anchor includes the fixed 1.5-s offset, giving the corresponding
marker-relative [2, 4) s segment. Trial eligibility and timing will not be
re-estimated from decoding outcomes.

Within each trial, each channel and view will be centered over time. A common
scale for all three views will be computed as the root-mean-square amplitude of
the centered broad view over its 18 channels and 320 samples. This within-trial
transformation yields dimensionless input and fits no population-level
normalization parameters. The new preprocessing runtime will use NumPy 2.2.6
and SciPy 1.15.3; prepared arrays and their metadata will receive recorded
hashes and a new experiment fingerprint. Preservation of the input definition
will not be interpreted as demonstrated bitwise equivalence with arrays
generated by a different historical runtime.

### Network and matched sampling

All methods will use the same compact EEGNet-style network implemented in
PyTorch. Its temporal block has eight filters with kernel length 64; its spatial
depth multiplier is two; the separable block has 16 outputs; dropout is 0.25;
and the classifier produces two logits. The implementation uses temporal and
depthwise spatial convolutions, BatchNorm, ELU activations, average pooling and
a final linear classifier.

Every optimization batch will include four participants from each source
cohort, both motor-imagery classes, and eight trials per class and participant,
giving 128 trials. The participant and trial sampling sequence will be matched
between methods at each seed. Training uses the broad, mu and beta views in
the same concatenated forward batch for all methods. Consequently, adding or
removing a consistency loss will not change the number of views observed by
BatchNorm. Inference will use only the broad view with fixed model parameters
and evaluation-mode BatchNorm.

### Participant–class objectives and ablations

Let \(\ell_g\) be the mean broad-view cross-entropy for an observed source
participant–class group \(g\) in the batch. For \(G\) observed groups, define

\[
L_{\mathrm{mean}}=\frac{1}{G}\sum_g\ell_g,
\qquad
L_{\mathrm{tail}}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{g\in\mathcal T_K}\ell_g,
\qquad K=\lceil0.5G\rceil,
\]

where \(\mathcal T_K\) contains the groups with the largest observed losses.
Groups absent from the batch will not be assigned artificial zero losses.
For trial \(i\), let \(p_{iv}\) be the temperature-1 predictive distribution
for view \(v\in\{b,\mu,\beta\}\), and let
\(\bar p_i=(p_{ib}+p_{i\mu}+p_{i\beta})/3\). The consistency term is

\[
L_{\mathrm{JS}}=\frac{1}{3B}\sum_{i=1}^{B}\sum_v
\operatorname{KL}(p_{iv}\Vert\bar p_i).
\]

All views retain gradients. The combined objective will be

\[
L=0.5L_{\mathrm{mean}}+0.5L_{\mathrm{tail}}+0.1L_{\mathrm{JS}}.
\]

Five matched methods will be evaluated: balanced ERM
(\(L_{\mathrm{mean}}\)), GroupDRO, tail-only
(\(0.5L_{\mathrm{mean}}+0.5L_{\mathrm{tail}}\)), consistency-only
(\(L_{\mathrm{mean}}+0.1L_{\mathrm{JS}}\)), and the combined objective.
The equal-weight tail mixture preserves the baseline loss scale. The finite
batch upper-tail term is an empirical risk surrogate rather than a guarantee
on an unobserved population's worst-case risk.

GroupDRO will operate on the same source participant–class groups. Its initial
weight for a group in cohort \(c\) with \(N_c\) training participants is
\(q_{0g}=1/(2CN_c)\), where \(C=2\) source cohorts. If four distinct people
are drawn from cohort \(c\), the inclusion probability is \(\pi_g=4/N_c\).
The model loss will use the pre-update group weights,
\(\sum_{g\in\mathcal B}q_g\ell_g/\pi_g\), followed by the exponential
weight update with step size 0.01 and normalization over all registered source
groups. Inclusion probabilities correct cohort-dependent sampling frequency.
The group weights and their sampling probabilities will be saved with the
optimizer checkpoint. The GroupDRO implementation is a matched baseline on the
present backbone; the underlying formulation is attributed to Sagawa et al.
[R5]. Upper-tail risk optimization is related to the CVaR literature [R6].

All trajectories will use Adam with learning rate 0.001, zero weight decay,
40 epochs and 128 updates per epoch. There is no learning-rate scheduler in
this fixed version, and automatic mixed precision is disabled. Loss
coefficients, epochs, seeds and methods will be fixed before the first fit.

### Participant-disjoint model selection

The 60 source participants will be divided into five outer folds, stratified
by dataset using split seed 20261023. In each outer training pool, a fixed
20% of participants within each cohort, rounded upward, will form the inner
validation set. The remaining people will fit model parameters. Each source
person will appear in one outer test fold. The five methods share these splits
and development seeds 20261031, 20261032 and 20261033, yielding 75 development
trajectories.

The inner selection score is weaker-class recall. For participant \(i\) and
seed \(s\), let \(r_{is,0}\) and \(r_{is,1}\) denote the two class recalls.
The score \(W_{is}=\min(r_{is,0},r_{is,1})\) will be averaged over people
within a cohort and then equally over cohorts. This avoids allowing the larger
Yang cohort to dominate selection by participant count alone. Equal scores
will select the earlier epoch. The outer participants will be scored using
that inner-selected checkpoint.

After all development trajectories finish, the integer median of each method's
15 inner-selected epochs will specify its final source-selected checkpoint.
Five methods and three new seeds, 20261101, 20261102 and 20261103, give
15 all-source final trajectories. Each will train to epoch 40 and retain the
selected checkpoint as well. Thus, fixed-40 and source-selected external
evaluation share fitted trajectories while separating common-duration
comparisons from method-specific duration selection. Outer-source and external
scores will not enter this selection rule.

### Outcomes and statistical analysis

Confusion counts will be combined across sessions for each person and seed
before calculating class recall. Balanced accuracy is
\(\mathrm{BA}_{is}=(r_{is,0}+r_{is,1})/2\). W and BA will first be calculated
within seed and then averaged across the three seeds for each person. The
participant, rather than a trial or a seed, is the unit of paired analysis.

The single primary comparison is combined objective minus ERM on W in the
nested outer-source evaluation. Its aggregate effect gives equal weight to
cohorts and then to participants within cohort. Four secondary comparisons per
domain will evaluate the combined objective against GroupDRO, tail-only and
consistency-only on W, and against ERM on BA; this fixed family will receive
Holm correction. The fixed-40 and source-selected Dreyer analyses will be
reported separately as exploratory external evaluations with equal participant
weights.

Pointwise 95% percentile intervals will use 10,000 paired participant
bootstrap samples, stratified within source cohort for the equal-cohort
estimand. Two-sided paired sign-flip tests will use exact enumeration for at
most 16 participants and otherwise 100,000 Monte Carlo sign patterns with a
plus-one correction. Reproducible resampling seeds are derived from base seed
20261024 and a fixed domain/contrast identifier. Method means, signed paired
effects, intervals, raw and adjusted p values, and improved/declined/unchanged
participant counts will all be retained. The intervals condition on the fitted
models; sign-flip inference assumes exchangeable signs. Shared training sets
across outer folds and historical exposure to Dreyer limit independent
confirmation and will be addressed in the study limitations.

### Execution, checkpointing and reproducibility

Independent method/seed/fold tasks will run as two single-GPU worker processes
inside one Slurm allocation on the school `hpc_gpu` partition. The enclosing
job requests one node, two GPUs (`gpu:2`), 26 CPUs and 128 GiB of shared
job memory. Independent workers correspond to 13 requested CPUs each. Raw
transport is staged on the authorized login host, while decompression,
preprocessing, training and evaluation share the offline compute allocation;
no training array is submitted. The implementation uses the allocated visible device, four PyTorch
intra-op threads, one inter-op thread, and no DataLoader subprocesses. Requested
resources will be distinguished from measured use in the completed report.
The actual GPU type and software/device history will be reported from allocation
records rather than inferred from the partition name.

Checkpoint files will preserve model and optimizer states, epoch and update
position, objective state, sampler and random-number states, best/selected
weights and validation history. Two checkpoint generations will be retained.
Time-based saves and preemption signals will permit interrupted fits to resume;
completed fits will be skipped. Per-session/run evaluation shards will permit
scoring to resume without repeating completed training. Actual optimizer steps,
processed samples and checkpoint records will document progress. A separate
GitHub synchronizer will publish status and completed summaries without
blocking model training when network access fails. Raw EEG and credentials
will not be included in those progress records.
